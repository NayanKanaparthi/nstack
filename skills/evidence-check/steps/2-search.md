# Step 2: Search both sides and save the ledger

Each hypothesis has four cells to fill:

| | For | Against |
|---|---|---|
| **Own words** (what people say and do) | search | search |
| **Numbers** (what someone counted) | search | search |

That is four searches per hypothesis. The limits are in `SKILL.md` under "Size of a run".

## Writing the searches

- Search for the problem and the people, not for the student's product.
- Make the "against" search as findable as the "for" search. Mirror the wording: "X is frustrating" against "X works fine for me", "can't find a good way to" against "I just use", "why I cancelled" against "worth paying for".
- Aim each "against" search at the "if false" line the student agreed to. For a hypothesis about how often something happens, that means people who don't have the problem, not a ranking of other problems.
- For numbers on a Problem or People hypothesis, "for" looks for how big or frequent the problem is. "Against" looks for how many don't have it. For a Status quo hypothesis, both look for counts of how many people use, pay for or have left the current way. Don't run a search whose results you wouldn't be able to file.
- Keep searches short and use the words users use, not founder words.
- At least one search per hypothesis names a place where these people write: a specific forum or community, or an existing product's name plus "reviews".
- Prefer the last five years. The audit sets aside anything older, so a page that turns out to be older than that counts as holding nothing useful.
- A second search for an empty cell changes the approach (a different place, a different kind of source), not just the wording. After that, record the cell as nothing found.

## What counts as evidence

1. **People's own words.** App and product reviews (App Store, Google Play, Trustpilot, G2, Capterra), question and forum threads (Quora, Hacker News, Stack Exchange, niche community forums), reader comments. Also a named person's own experience in a news article, quoted or reported.
2. **Numbers from whoever counted them.** Government statistics, academic papers, surveys that state their method, company filings.
3. **Organisation facts.** What a company, university or other organisation says about itself: its price, its features, its own programme, its shutdown notice.

What doesn't count, on either side:

- Anyone describing what other people feel or do. That covers the writer's own voice in news articles and blogs, an organisation's page when it talks about its users and not about itself, and a person talking about someone else.
- Anyone who gains from the reader's choice: a professional writer reviewing or recommending a product, a comment carrying the writer's own referral link, a seller describing what they are selling.
- Customer quotes a seller chose to show on its own page.
- "Top 10" lists, statistics roundup pages, press releases for paid market reports and AI-written summaries. If a roundup cites a number, follow it to the original. If you can't reach the original, don't use the number.

**Reddit** often can't be opened. If a search shows a Reddit thread that looks relevant and you can't open it, don't quote it. List it under "Not opened" so the student can open it themselves and paste it in.

## Scan first, then narrow

Run all four searches for a hypothesis before opening anything. Look across the results, then choose up to four pages to open, in this order of preference: a person's own account, then an original number, then an organisation's own page. Don't spend a page on a kind of source that can't count. If both sides returned results, open at least one page from each side.

Opening the first good-looking result on one side before the other side has been searched is how a run ends up lopsided.

Relevant pages you didn't open, because they were blocked or you had reached the limit, go under "Not opened" with a note of what they seemed to hold and which side they seemed to be on.

## Taking items from a page

An item is one quote, one number or one organisation fact. Take up to three items per page for each hypothesis. A quote that speaks to both hypotheses is filed under each.

- Take the ones that speak most directly to the hypothesis, and prefer ones where the person did something about it. Don't pick items to even out the two sides. If a page leans one way, the ledger should show that.
- On the first item from each page, note what else was there and which way it leaned for each hypothesis: for example "about 10 other relevant comments, most of them against H1".
- Copy quotes exactly, typos included, one to three sentences. Don't join separate sentences into one quote. Before saving, check each quote against the page once more.
- Record who said it only as the page states it. If an article reports what a named person did, say "as reported by" and name the outlet. Record whether the page shows they belong to the group that counts, and how.
- For a number, record who counted it and what exactly was counted.
- For own words, record the action (see `SKILL.md`).
- An item goes in the cell that matches its own side and kind, whichever search found it. Organisation facts go with numbers.
- Some numbers measure the very thing the hypothesis is about without settling it. Don't force a side. File them with the side `context` (see `SKILL.md`).

If the student pastes evidence in themselves, such as a Reddit thread, add it as an item marked `pasted by student`.

While you read, note up to four existing products or services that already do part of what the idea does. Record the name, a link to its own page or store listing, and one fact that page states: its price, what it does, its download count, its star rating. A reviewer's opinion of a product is not a fact about it. Don't go looking for more products; this is not a competitor scan.

## Save the ledger

Create a file called `evidence-to-check.md` with exactly this layout. The checking half reads it, so keep the headings as they are. It is working material: the student carries it to the next step and never needs to read it. If you can't create files, print the whole ledger in one code block and ask the student to copy it.

```
# Evidence ledger

Made with: evidence-check
Date: <today>
Run size: quick | light | full
Status: not yet audited

## Idea
<as confirmed>

## Who counts
<as confirmed>

## Hypotheses
### H1 (<type>)
<statement, as confirmed>
- If true, we'd expect to find: ...
- If false, we'd expect to find: ...

## For interviews only
- I1: <statement> | <reason, as confirmed>

## Not checked this time
- <any other searchable hypothesis, as confirmed, or "none">

## Searches
| # | Hyp | Side | Kind | Search words | What came back |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| S1 | H1 | for | own words | ... | 2 usable pages |
| S2 | H1 | against | own words | ... | nothing usable |

## Evidence
### E1
- Hypothesis: H1
- Side: for | against | context
- Kind: own words | number | organisation fact
- Quote: "..."
- Link: https://...
- Who: <as the page states; for a number, who counted and what was counted>
- When: <date on the page, or "no date shown">
- Where: <site and kind of page, e.g. "Trustpilot review">
- Group shown: yes, <how the page shows it> | no
- Action: <what they did> | none        (own words only)
- Opened: yes | snippet only | pasted by student
- Also on this page: <what else relevant was there, and which way it leaned>
- Note: <anything a checker should know, or leave this line out>

## Existing products and services that came up
- <name> | <link to its own page or listing> | <one fact that page states>

## Nothing found
- H1, against, numbers: tried "<words>" and "<words>"

## Not opened
- <link> | <blocked, or over the page limit> | <what it seemed to hold, and for which side>
```

If you opened a page and it held nothing useful, say so in the Searches table.

## Hand over

Tell the student how much was collected, in plain words. Give counts only: don't describe any finding and don't say how good the evidence looks. Interpretation waits for the audit, because unchecked evidence read aloud starts to sound like a conclusion.

- **With sub-agents:** say one line ("Collected. Checking it now."), launch the checking sub-agent as described in `SKILL.md`, and share only the page it returns.
- **In a normal chat**, send something like:

```
Done collecting. Nothing here has been checked yet, so don't rely on it.

- <n> searches, <n> pages read
- <first hypothesis in a few words>: <n> items for, <n> against
- <second hypothesis in a few words>: <n> items for, <n> against
- Nothing turned up for: <plain description, e.g. "figures on how many owners pay a walker">
- Filed as background, for neither side: <n>
- Pages found but not read: <n>, of which <n> wouldn't open (they are listed in the file)

Next: download evidence-to-check.md (no need to read it), open a **new chat**, attach it and type: check this
```
