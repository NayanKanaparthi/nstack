# Calibration: worked pairs

Every check reads this file together with `checking-rules.md`, and so does the audit. Each pair holds one statement and two items: one that counts and one that doesn't, with the one-line reason a careful, fair reader would give. Calibrate on these, not on words like "strict" or "reasonable".

The quotes below are made up to show the rules. They are never evidence, and never go into a ledger, a note or a page.

## 1. Ordinary inference counts; a real guess doesn't

Statement: "Allotment holders log their harvests in garden apps."

- **Counts, for.** "Opened the app tonight to add the courgettes and beans I picked last week." The group is shown by the review's other line, "I've used it on every allotment plot I've had." Reason: entering what one picked from one's plot in the app is logging a harvest in an app. That is ordinary inference.
- **Set aside, `indirect`.** "Great app, I use it for everything on the plot." Reason: whether "everything" includes logging what was picked is a guess the page doesn't settle.

## 2. A detail doesn't block counting; a different situation does

Statement: "Freelance designers chase clients for late invoice payments."

- **Counts, for.** "Spent all of Monday emailing three clients about invoices that were weeks overdue." The page shows she is a freelance designer. Reason: chasing late payment is the core claim. No amount, client type or exact delay is needed: those are details.
- **Set aside, `indirect`.** "My employer paid my salary two weeks late and HR ignored my emails." Reason: a salary from an employer is a different situation from invoices to clients.

## 3. An against item counts at the same level as a for item

Statement: "Parents struggle to find a babysitter at short notice."

- **Counts, for.** "Called six sitters when my shift changed that afternoon, nobody was free."
- **Counts, against.** "When my shift changes last minute I text the sitter group chat and someone always answers within the hour." Reason: the plain words settle the claim the other way for this parent, in the same situation. It needs no more detail than the for item did.

## 4. Silence is not the opposite

Statement: "Dog owners who work long hours pay for midday walks."

- **Counts, against.** "I'm out ten hours a day and I just come home at lunch to walk her, I've never paid anyone." Reason: long hours are shown, and her own words say she doesn't pay for walks.
- **Set aside, `indirect`.** "I work long hours and my dog is the best part of my day." Reason: it says nothing about midday walks or paying. A quote that is silent on the claim doesn't show its opposite.

## 5. The group shown by context

Statement: "Small bakery owners throw away unsold bread at the end of the day." (`who_counts`: people who run a bakery)

- **Counts, for.** A review of a bakery point-of-sale app: "Our end-of-day waste report shows we binned 40 loaves this week, mostly sourdough." Reason: a review of a bakery till app that talks about "our" loaves shows a careful reader this writer runs a bakery.
- **Set aside, `group_not_shown`.** On a general cooking forum: "We throw out so much bread every evening, it kills me." Reason: nothing on the page shows whether "we" is a bakery, a café or a household, and a careful reader can't tell.

## 6. A stake shown, and a stake suspected

Statement: "Cyclists who commute in winter pay for waterproof gear."

- **Set aside, `stake`.** "I paid for the Storm jacket and it kept me dry all January (use my code for 20% off, I get a cut)." Reason: the page shows the writer gains from the reader's purchase.
- **Counts, for.** "Bought proper overtrousers after my third soaked commute this December." A reply below says "sounds like an ad". Reason: the suspicion is someone else's and nothing on the page confirms a gain. The note says others suspected an advert.

## 7. A passing mention, and a product recommendation

Statement: "Nurses on night shifts skip meals during their shift."

- **Counts, for.** A nurse's personal blog post: "Most nights I don't eat until 7 am. I've started keeping protein bars from the corner shop in my locker." Reason: a personal account that names what she uses is not a product recommendation.
- **Set aside, `stake`.** A staff writer's "Five best snack boxes for night-shift nurses" article: "As a former nurse I skipped every meal on nights, which is why I love Box A." Reason: the piece's purpose is recommending products, so a professional writer's own experience in it doesn't count.

## 8. An old item, and a current one

Statement: "Students buy second-hand textbooks to save money." Today's date: 2026-10-16.

- **Counts, for.** Dated 2024-09-02: "Got all four of my set books used for a third of the shop price."
- **Set aside, `old`.** Dated 2019-08-30: "Bought every textbook second-hand this year, saved about £200." Reason: more than five years before today. It speaks to the claim, but it is too old to count.

## 9. A number about the group, and a number about a wider group

Statement: "Independent restaurants run out of key ingredients during service."

- **Counts, for (number).** A trade association's survey of 412 independent restaurant owners, which says who counted and when: "62% ran out of at least one menu ingredient mid-service in the past month." The association sells nothing for stock-keeping. Reason: an original count of this group, on this claim.
- **Set aside, `indirect`.** "Half of all hospitality businesses report supply problems." Reason: a wider group (all hospitality), and "supply problems" is a wider claim than running out mid-service.

## 10. An organisation that sells a fix

Statement: "Homeowners wait more than a week for a plumber."

- **Counts as background (side `context`).** A plumbing booking app's own survey: "41% of our users waited over 7 days before booking through us." Reason: an organisation counting the problem it sells a fix for gives background at most. The note says what it sells.
- **Counts, for.** A consumer charity's survey of 1,200 homeowners, which sells nothing in plumbing: "One in three waited more than a week for a plumber last year." Reason: an original count, on this claim, from an organisation with nothing to sell for it.

## 11. Says both things

Statement: "Gym members find peak-hour crowding frustrating."

- **Background (side `context`).** "The 6 pm rush drives me mad, but honestly I love the buzz, it gets me going." Reason: one part shows the claim true for this writer and the other its plain opposite.
- **Counts, for.** "The 6 pm rush drives me mad, so I've moved my sessions to 9 pm." Reason: frustration, plus what she did about it. Wanting a quieter time is not the opposite.

## 12. The kind of thing acted on

Statement (`today`): "Self-published authors pay a designer for their book covers." (`who_counts`: authors who publish their own books)

- **Counts, for.** "Paid a designer $300 for the cover of my second novel, and it was worth every cent." The page shows she publishes her own books. Reason: the same action, on the kind of thing the statement names.
- **Set aside, `indirect`.** "I pay freelancers for everything around my books: editing, formatting, the lot." Note: "Doesn't say a cover was among them." Reason: the same action on a wider kind of thing. Whether it includes covers is a guess the page doesn't settle.
