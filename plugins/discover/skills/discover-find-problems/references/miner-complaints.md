# Miner 1: complaints and frustrations

Read `mining-rules.md` in this same folder first. It holds the rules for what counts, your budget and how to log what you find. This brief tells you where to look. Your source name in the log is `complaints`, so your ids start with `K`.

## Your job

Find people in the given area describing, in their own words, something that frustrates or costs them. You look at what people complain about. The other two miners cover workarounds and spending, and organisations' own numbers, so leave those to them unless they turn up on a page you are already reading.

## Where to look

- Forums and community threads where these people write: niche forums, Hacker News, Stack Exchange sites, Quora, reader comments.
- Reviews with 1 to 3 stars of products and services these people already use: App Store, Google Play, Trustpilot, G2, Capterra. Low-star reviews show what goes wrong. Use 4 and 5 star reviews only as items against a candidate, if they say the thing works fine.
- Question sites where people ask for help with the problem itself.

## How to search

- Use the words these people use, not founder words. Mix the people or the part of life with frustration words: "<people> frustrating", "hate <task>", "worst part of <activity>", "anyone else struggle with <task>", "<task> takes forever".
- At least two searches name a place: a specific forum or community, or an existing product's name plus "reviews".
- At least one search goes to low-star reviews: "<product> 1 star review", "<product> reviews complaints".
- A second try on an empty search changes the approach (a different place, a different kind of source), not just the wording.

## What to record

Mostly own-words items. The usual signals here are `complaint`, `time spent` and `asked for recommendations`, but set each one from what the quote shows.

Follow `mining-rules.md` to log what you find as you go, with `--source complaints`.
