# Miner 2: workarounds and spending

Read `mining-rules.md` in this same folder first. It holds the rules for what counts, your budget and how to log what you find. This brief tells you where to look. Your source name in the log is `workarounds`, so your ids start with `W`.

## Your job

Find signs that people in the given area already put money or effort into a problem: makeshift fixes, paid tools, people hired to do it, people switching or cancelling. Doing costs something, so it tells more than complaining. The other two miners cover complaints, and organisations' own numbers.

## Where to look

- "How do I" questions where people describe what they do now: spreadsheets, scripts, reminders, asking a friend, doing it by hand.
- People describing paying for help, switching from one option to another, or cancelling one: "I pay for", "switched from", "why I cancelled", "ended up hiring".
- Paid tools and services for the problem: their own pricing or product page. Record what the page states it charges and for what, as an organisation fact. A seller's description of its own product counts only for that fact, never as proof that people need it.
- Job posts: an organisation's own post showing it pays someone to do the task by hand. Record it as an organisation fact: who is the organisation, `did` is the task and the pay if the post states it. Never record the name or contact details of the person who posted it.

## How to search

- Use the words these people use: "how do I <task>", "<task> spreadsheet", "I pay someone to <task>", "switched from <product>", "cancelled <product>", "<task> job" or "<task> assistant hiring".
- At least one search names a place where these people write.
- At least one search looks for job posts for the task.
- A second try on an empty search changes the approach, not just the wording.

## What to record

The usual signals here are `workaround`, `money spent`, `time spent` and `switched`, but set each one from what the quote shows. A pricing page shows what a product charges, not that anyone pays it: its signal is `none` unless the page itself shows people paying.

Follow `mining-rules.md` to log what you find as you go, with `--source workarounds`.
